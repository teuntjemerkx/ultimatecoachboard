# Handoff: where the project stands

Read this first when you continue the project in a new session or under another account.

The owner speaks Dutch: always answer in Dutch. App UI text, code, comments and commits are in English.

## What this repo holds

- `CoachBoardCore/` is the Swift package with all the logic: match, rotations, zones, 3D and video. It has 132 tests. Run them without a Mac via Docker:
  ```
  docker run --rm --network none -v "$PWD/CoachBoardCore:/pkg" -w /pkg swift:6.1-noble swift test
  ```
- `App/` is the native iPad app (SwiftUI + Metal). It builds in GitHub Actions only (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, macOS runner). Screenshots land on the `ci-screenshots` branch.
  - The owner does **not** want a paid App Store app.
  - The native app is complete through phase 2, but it is no longer the focus.
- `web/` is **the product the owner uses**: a free, installable web app (PWA) for the iPad.
  - Stack: Vite + React + TypeScript (strict), Three.js, zustand, IndexedDB, and MediaPipe for video.
  - Hosting: Vercel project `coach-board`, connected to GitHub with root directory `web`. Every push to `main` deploys to production at https://coach-board-teuntjemerkx-8584s-projects.vercel.app (public; previews need a Vercel login).
  - To install, open the link in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen.
  - All the owner's data stays on the iPad (IndexedDB). Settings → Backup makes a JSON file.

## Web app: done

- The look (`web/src/app/styles.css`, `theme.ts`): the owner's Prizm design (trading cards). The default theme "Prizm" is near-black ink (#0D0C18) with a violet glow, indigo cards (#1E1E37), violet for the one big action (#9C85FF, pill buttons), gold small lettering (#EACC88), Bebas Neue for big headings and numbers; the teams are violet (us, #9C85FF) against gold (them, #E8B84A) on the court, in 3D and on the filled point buttons (apart on the blue–yellow axis, so safe for colour blindness); the boards show a lit court in a dark arena. "Game day" (navy locker room, blue LED strips) and "Locker room" (the same by day, for a bright hall) remain with three more themes; users on an earlier default move to Prizm once. Players are 3D trading cards (`ui/PrizmCard.tsx`): a deep prism burst on foil with fine rainbow diffraction lines from the light, a refraction that slides as the card turns, glare and print grain, the card in a clear plastic case; no figure on the card, the name fills it. Pressed, a card tilts toward the finger; a tap turns it over (Players: its facts and Edit, which loads it into the form with Save and Remove) or picks it (New match). Finishes: prizm, chrome (a player already on court), gold (the libero), base (an empty card). Every screen fills the iPad held either way with big cards and buttons (52 pt at least, the main one 72 pt) and nothing to scroll or swipe (`.fill` grids; card racks size their cards to fit the whole team, `useFitColumns`; a phone falls back to one scrolling column): Home (the next match and Start/Resume, four lockers: Studio, Video to 3D, Players with a fan of the team's cards, Season), Settings (Team, Schedule, Look, Backup, About), Players (the team's cards, a new card filling in as you type), Season (four numbers, side-out per rotation, matches a page at a time), New match (the lineup as six foil slots, the team's cards to pick from, stepping through the empty positions), the live scoreboard with big point buttons, the match log per set, and Video to 3D's first step. Team-coloured buttons use `onHome`/`onOpponent` for their text.
- Navigation and the live screen (research-led redesign, 2026-10-17): a tab bar (`ui/Tabs.tsx`, in `ui/Bar.tsx`) with Match, Team, Studio, Video, Season and Settings, one tap each; screens inside a section get a back button. The Match tab (`screens/Home.tsx`) shows the match in play or the next one large (score or start time), with Coming up and Results beside it. Live has no tab bar and never changes shape: the score line, a row of counters in fixed places (time-outs and substitutions used per team, amber on the last time-out and the last two substitutions, the current run, the lead), the court and the big buttons (two points, two time-outs, Sub, Undo). Tapping a player on the court starts a substitution with that player going off (2 taps). A time-out opens a view with the 30 s clock, both rotations, side-out per rotation with counts and the coach's notes on the opponent (`match/notes.ts`, written at setup, carried in backups). The match log is reached from the time-out view, the set break and the Match and Season tabs. Team adds and edits cards in a sheet. Season shows each percentage with its counts. Basis: FIVB rules 15.1/15.4.1/18.1, touch-target research (NN/g, Parhi/Karlson), glanceability (Berkeley 2006); see the deep-research report in the session.
- Match day safety (`store/db.ts`, `match/session.ts`): IndexedDB is opened again when iOS drops the connection, writes settle on the transaction (a full disk rejects), Live shows "Not saved · tap to try again" when a save fails and stores everything when the app goes to the background; the Studio saves on hide too. Backups carry the localStorage settings; a restore checks the whole file, merges (keeps clips, the longer match journal, the newer board) in one transaction, asks first and restarts the app. The match log goes back to a match in play and deletes a match (tap twice); the match-over sheet can undo the last point; picking the libero takes that player off court.
- Speed and battery on the iPad (branch `perf-ipad`, measured in the Claude browser pane on an M3 against commit 0a6093b; the iPad is likely 2 to 3 times slower):
  - Studio edits no longer wait for the body separation. The rally's `SeparationTrack` is solved in a module worker (`studio/bodies.worker.ts`, built by `studio/bodyTrack.ts`, the same code the page used) and sent back in slices; the page reads the moments with `SeparationMoments` (`core/spatial/bodySeparation.ts`). The track is kept per `bodyKey` (players without their names, timeline, contacts, landing, setup, women/men, athletes), so ink, zones, the title and names never solve the rally again. Until the moment shown is solved, the last moment solved is used, eased back toward the paths; when it arrives the players move over in 0.35 s (both pictures kept apart, so nothing jumps or overlaps). Without a worker the track is solved on the page, 4 ms after each frame starts. A player moved at Attack: 1.6 to 1.7 s blocked before, 22 to 26 ms now (no task over 50 ms), the rally solved again in about 0.7 s; ink 1.1 s before, 13 to 24 ms now. The bodies are the same to the micrometre (`bodyTrack.test.ts`, and both builds compared moment by moment).
  - The solver itself is about 1.9 times faster with the same results: no allocation in the capsule distance loop, an exact bounding-box cull per body, the athlete rig's constants made once (`athleteBody.ts`); ball flights are remembered by their inputs (`FlightSolver`), so an edit that leaves a flight's ends in place does not shoot it again (50 ms to 0.2 ms).
  - The Studio's controls pick their fields from the store (`useStudioFields`, zustand `useShallow`); the 3D board takes the playhead through `useStudio.subscribe`, not React. During playback only the board and the marker chips change. Main thread busy during a rally: 39 to 45 % before, 16 to 19 % now.
  - `Board3D` draws only when something changed (`invalidate`: players, camera, flights, resize); an idle board draws nothing (it drew every frame, about 250 draw calls). Shadows are drawn again only when players move (`shadowMap.autoUpdate` off; the map stays 2048, at 1024 the shadows of legs and arms fade). Pixel ratio at most 1.5. Eyes, brows and lashes cast no shadow. Each board has its own canvas and hands its WebGL context back on dispose. `Players3D` rebuilds zones, ink, sight lines, highlights and ball paths only when they change, shares zone pattern textures (they leaked before), reads the theme's CSS colours once per theme and poses only players posed anew. `WatchBoard` polls its view each frame (`onFrame` returns true) and draws on change; `Hall3D` wakes the board when the plate loads.
  - Prizm cards no longer drift their light for ever (11 s, all card layers repainted every frame): each card rests where the drift would have started it, follows the finger while pressed and glides back. The fan on Home has a box shadow instead of a drop-shadow filter. Idle Home: nothing animates.
- Loading and updates (`App.tsx`, `updates.ts`, `vite.config.ts`): Studio and Video to 3D are lazy chunks (the start chunk is about 295 KB); MediaPipe (34 MB) is cached on first use of Video to 3D, not precached (precache about 5.5 MB). The service worker is in prompt mode: a new version waits and Home shows "New version · restart"; it checks for updates when the app comes to the front, and a missing chunk after an update reloads once.
- Matches:
  - Setup, the live court with one-tap points, undo and swap sides.
  - Substitutions, the libero and time-outs on Live (`screens/SubSheet.tsx`, `screens/Live.tsx`). Sub opens a sheet: the six on court as cards in their court layout, then the bench (the team's players not in the lineup at the start, plus starters who went off). The FIVB rules are the core's (`MatchState.substitutionRefusal`, which `substitute` uses too): 6 per set, a substitute only for the starter they replaced, a starter back only in their own position, then the pair is closed. A refused card is plain with the rule on its tab ("Played", "For #8", "Stays on"), and a tap on it explains in full. The libero changes by itself (the core's rule); on the court the libero carries a "for 12" tag, and the sheet says whom the libero plays for. Time-out per team (2 per set, lit dots in the team colour) with a 30 s clock over the court. Time-outs are a web addition to the core: `LiveEvent.timeOut` / `LiveAction.timeOut`, journalled as TimeOutStarted and replayed, counted in `TeamInSet.timeOutsUsed`; the panic time-out counts as a home time-out. Undo takes back points, subs and time-outs alike and says under the button what it will take back (`LiveState.lastAction`).
  - New match takes their lineup (optional, "Their lineup" beside who serves): their six numbers by position on a number pad that steps through positions 1 to 6, and their setter's position. Their tokens on Live then carry their numbers.
  - Substitutions for both teams: Us | Them at the top of the Sub sheet (the chosen team lit in its colour, each with its substitutions left). Them: their six as gold cards (their numbers from setup, else the role), then their players who went off (a returning starter) beside a big number pad for the incoming shirt number. Us: "Not in the team?" on the bench step opens the same pad beside the bench. A substitute going off shows only the bench (their starter is the one who may return), no pad. A number the match already knows for that team is that player (`LiveState.playerWithNumber`, so their #15 in set 2 is the same #15 as in set 1); a new number gets a new id, and its number (and an optional name) travel in the substitution itself: `SubstitutionPayload.number`/`name` and the same on `LiveEvent`/`LiveAction` (`_3`/`_4`), all optional, so old journals replay unchanged. `LiveState.newcomers` is derived from the active log, so undo takes a typed number away with its substitution; `jersey`, `playerName`, `sideOf` and `bench(side)` read it, and `MatchSession.number`/`label`/`who` go through them (who names their unnumbered players by role). Undo says "Their #22 for #7".
  - Set break with side-out by rotation and the next start rotation.
  - Match log: a card per rally; the substitutions and time-outs before a rally show under it as marks in the team's colour, with a count (`MatchLogRow.interruptions`, a web addition beside the counts).
  - Saved after every point as the binary journal (`web/src/app/match/session.ts`).

      - The app draws the kit double-sided (the inside shows through a sleeve), hair, brows and lashes with alpha-to-coverage, and keeps every texture (the shorts' too: its occlusion, under the theme's shorts colour). `tools/athletes/poses.html` shows both athletes in a row of postures in the team colours (`?focus=` for a close-up; `window.scene` for the console).
    - Nobody stands in anyone (`core/spatial/bodySeparation.ts`, used by `studioBodies` and `videoBodies`): capsule bodies slid apart on the floor, the trunk and head never through the net. A `SeparationTrack` per rally solves moment by moment (so players step around each other instead of jumping), looks one second ahead along the real paths so teammates about to run into each other drift apart in time (the room needed is measured on the bodies and opens over the last 0.6 s before they meet, at most 4 m/s), is read averaged over 0.1 s either side (the moment's own separation clears what that lets through), and is solved ahead in the background (`prepare`). A player taking steps is kept apart by the whole step cycle (`PosedPlayer.strideEnvelope`: its turning points, 1.5 cm thicker for the arcs between them, as a long arm sags that far between two), so swinging arms and legs never reach into anyone and nobody is pushed about at every step. Checked over every system, rotation, serve and attack at 60 fps (a scratch scan, not committed): no overlap of the drawn bodies, no trunk through the net.
    - How a scene plays (`core/zones/sceneMotion.ts`): a phase takes 1.1 s, longer when someone has farther to run than an average 4.5 m/s (a 6.8 m/s peak) allows (`sceneClock`); players moving between markers take steps (`spatial/gait.ts`); while the serve is in the air the server's teammates watch where it goes, not the server behind them.
    - Players meet without pops (`relax` in `bodySeparation.ts`, branch `separation-pops`). A pop was a player visibly jumping sideways in one frame; four causes, each fixed. (1) A push along the chosen way (the line between the pelvises, leaned sideways when they run into each other) can only clear them far off when an arm or leg lies across the other's, seen from above: it is dragged along its whole length (the men's libero overtaking her outside hitter, rotation 5 right: 38 cm where 2 cm sideways did). For a push over 4 cm the solver now also tries ways turned 22.5 to 90 degrees to either side and takes the shortest (turning counts against it). (2) The sideways lean halves on every round of the solver, so a player caught between two others is pushed out between them instead of sliding off a little further on every round (three players crossing in front of the net). (3) A pass whose paths turn (a setter setting off) now passes on the side the paths give, instead of being swung a metre across in one moment to the side chosen before. (4) The step cycle's turning points (`strideEnvelope`) do not hold the whole cycle: between them a swinging foot reaches up to 25 cm outside, a hand up to 13 cm. The poses of the moment are now kept apart as well (pose against pose only, so nobody is pushed about at every step); the shorter pushes of (1) had shown 1 cm overlaps of feet. Scan of every rotation and attack, both teams serving, women and men, at 60 fps, with today's bodies and with the athletes-refresh bodies (a scratch scan, not committed): no overlap, and nobody moves farther in a 60th of a second than 1.5 times their own path plus 10 cm (at least 2.8 cm under that; before, up to 15 cm over it in rotation 5 middle, and 2.5 to 8 cm over at the start of rotations 2, 3 and 6). With the Studio's own weights (0.3 + speed) the old solver went up to 41 cm over (rotations 1 and 4 at the start, 5 middle); now nothing is over, one moment is at the limit: the men's rotation 1 middle at 0.6 s with the athletes-refresh bodies, where three players crossing at the net hold two of them up to 2 m back along their paths before they pass. The rally test (`bodySeparation.test.ts`) now plays one rally per rotation, every attack twice, both teams serving (12 rallies, about 30 s). Solving a rally takes about 15 % longer (the poses of the moment).

  - `pipeline.ts` (`buildRally`): pose frames at 10 per second from `clip.ts` (a hidden `<video>`, at most 1920 px; an all-black frame right after a seek is read again), `detect.ts` on each (the pose model only for people standing near the court, from their box through the camera), the jersey colour of each person as the tracker's appearance cue. Between each two pose frames two more for the ball alone (`samplePlan`, 30 frames a second in all; `Detector.detectBalls` runs the object detector's two main tiles without the zoomed band and the pose crops, about a fifth of a pose frame's time). A frame that repeats the one read before (a clip with fewer frames per second) is skipped by a 480 px fingerprint (`sameFrame`: fewer than one pixel in 2000 differs by more than 8 levels, so a repeat that a re-encode to 30 fps added, with a few levels of compression noise, counts too; a new frame differs in about one pixel in a hundred); a repeated pose frame keeps its people but not its ball. From which reads repeated, `frameGrid` finds the frame rate and phase of a clip with fewer frames than are read (23.976, 24, 25 or 29.97 fps), and the ball is timed when its frame came up (`onFrameGrid`), not at the read time; the players keep the read times (see "Video to 3D: what is left"). One ball pixel per frame over all of them (`pickBalls`): candidates at the same pixel in four or more frames over a second or more, or in five or more frames over 0.2 s without a break of 0.1 s, are left out first (`withoutFixtures`: an antenna top, a logo, a spare ball, a head standing still), then the candidate nearest to where the path leads is taken, down to the detector's threshold, and a new path starts only at a score of 0.2; a pick off the path starts a new path. Each ball candidate keeps its box's smaller side as the ball's diameter when no frame or tile edge cuts the box (`FrameDetections.balls[].size`, one candidate per ball where the tiles overlap: `oneBallEach`), and it reaches the core as `VideoFrameObservation.ballSize`. About 24 pose frames with people masked for the clean plate. The plate and the reconstruction run in `rebuild.worker.ts` (a module worker). In dev, `globalThis.__videoDebug` keeps the last observations, the reads, the frame grid and the ball candidates of every frame (with sizes and read times), for replays in Node.
  - `rallyTracker.ts` (`RallyTracker.forCamera`): the core's tracker with two rules for real footage: tracks seen inside the lines in fewer than 3 frames are dropped before the six-per-side limit (coaches, line judges, officials), and the jump allowance (`maxFootHeight`) is cut to about 2 m along the court for a far, low camera (from 45 to 12 home detections across the net on the broadcast clip). Each player's stature (women/men from Settings) is stored with the scene.

  - How the players move (web additions in `VideoFrame.posedPlayers`): the posture library has no running or walking, and on real footage the fitted posture is mostly a guess (OK Hoče clip: mean confidence 0.04 to 0.25 per posture; a "set" or "sprawl" held for seconds while the player runs 5 m/s, because samples without keypoints keep the last posture). So a grounded posture under confidence 0.4 fades to "ready" (fully under 0.2), and a moving player takes steps: `core/video/videoMotion.ts` reads each track's velocity over 0.2 s either side (capped at 7 m/s) and advances a step phase with the distance covered; `core/spatial/gait.ts` (`strideParams`) lays the cycle over the pose (feet alternating along the travel, half steps of 0.12 + 0.12 m per m/s up to 0.55 m, the swinging foot lifted, arms against the legs, the trunk leaning into a forward run), faded in from 0.4 to 1.2 m/s, never while airborne. The legs reach their targets by `SkeletonModel.reachFeet` (the IK split out of `solveStance`). The planted foot moves under 2 cm while the body passes it by a metre (`videoMotion.test.ts`). `posedPlayers({ stride: false })` leaves the steps out.

The clean plate is still made and stored (the video view shows it when there is no clip).
