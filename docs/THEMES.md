# Colour and layout: research notes and the five templates

Settings > Appearance offers five templates. **Sports hall** is the default and the recommendation for live use.
This note records why. Sources are listed at the end. Where the evidence is thin, it says so.

## Findings

1. **Halls are bright, and in bright light dark-on-light reads better.**
   - Competition halls are lit at 300–750 lux, per the EN 12193 classes: III 300, II 500, I 750, as quoted by lighting manufacturers.
   - In proofreading studies, positive polarity (dark text on a light background) gave smaller pupils and better performance than negative polarity, for younger and older adults alike (Piepenbrock, Mayr, Buchner, *Ergonomics* 2013 and 2014).
   - The advantage grows as characters get smaller (*Human Factors*).
   - Not every study agrees: a 2024 study found faster reading with negative polarity. Dark themes stay useful in dim rooms, which is why there are two dark templates.
2. **Team colours must survive colour-vision deficiency.**
   - About 8% of men have a red-green deficiency.
   - Blue against orange is the pair most often recommended as safe across deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia (Okabe-Ito palette: blue #0072B2, orange #E69F00).
   - Pink against cyan (the original Neon look) is weaker for some deficiencies.
   - The board never relies on colour alone: home tokens are circles and opponents are diamonds.
3. **Contrast.**
   - Apple's accessibility criteria point to WCAG: 4.5:1 for text.
   - Common guidance adds 3:1 for large text and controls.
   - Each template was checked; the script lives in the session notes. Examples:
     - Sports hall: text 16.4:1, blue team on the court 5.4:1, orange 3.5:1, lines 13.3:1.
     - TV broadcast: the court was darkened to #B8501F so the white lines reach 5.0:1.
4. **Courts.**
   - FIVB rules ask for a light-coloured playing court, a free zone in a different colour, and white lines.
   - The TV broadcast template follows that look.
5. **Layout.**
   - Tablet grips vary more than phone grips, and 88% of tablet use is seated (Hoober and Shank). The sideline is the exception, so primary actions sit at the screen edges near the grip, never in the middle.
   - The old layout used only about 30% of a portrait screen for the court. In portrait the court now stands upright (Settings > Court: "Fill the screen").
   - The score is now 28–40 pt depending on the template, up from 20 pt, with team names in team colours.
   - The start screen is an adaptive grid with one primary action.

## The templates

| Template | For | Ground | Teams | Actions | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sports hall (recommended) | Live in a lit hall | Light | Blue vs orange (colour-blind-safe) | Ink black | SF Pro |
| Evening | Dim rooms, video, night | Dark | Sky blue vs orange | Light grey | SF Pro |
| TV broadcast | Presenting, the "TV" feel | Navy, terracotta court, blue free zone | Yellow vs white | Yellow | SF Pro Condensed, 40 pt score |
| Whiteboard | Familiar for coaches and players | White | Red vs blue markers | Ink black | SF Pro Rounded |
| Neon | The original look | Deep navy | Pink vs cyan | Pink | SF Pro Expanded |
